# Maintenance Troubleshooting: Tripped Breaker / Dead Outlet / Partial Power Loss

**Trigger phrases:** power is out in one room, outlet not working, breaker tripped, no power to part of the apartment, some lights not working, outlet dead, GFCI not resetting.

## Step 1 — Check the breaker panel

Ask the tenant to locate their electrical breaker panel and look for a breaker that's sitting in the middle position, or not fully aligned with the others (a tripped breaker often looks "off-center" rather than simply flipped to off).

If they find one: have them flip it **fully to OFF, then back to ON** to reset it.

## Step 2 — Check for a GFCI outlet

If the affected outlet is in a kitchen, bathroom, or outdoors, it may be (or be connected to) a GFCI outlet — these have their own small "Reset" button built into the outlet face. Ask the tenant to press it.

## If the breaker trips again immediately

**Stop here — do not keep resetting it.** A breaker that trips again right away means there's a real electrical problem, not a simple reset situation. Tell the tenant not to keep flipping it, and escalate.

## Do not

- Do not have the tenant reset a breaker more than once if it trips again immediately.
- If there's any burning smell, visible sparking, scorch marks, or smoke near the panel or outlet: this is not a routine issue — stop troubleshooting and follow the Universal Emergency Protocol instead (treat as a fire/smoke hazard).
- Do not suggest opening the breaker panel cover, touching wiring, or anything beyond flipping a breaker switch or pressing a GFCI reset button.

## If it's not resolved

If resetting the breaker or GFCI doesn't restore power, or it re-trips: stop troubleshooting, tell the tenant you're getting this to the maintenance team, and hand off to Roy (transfer + internal notification, per the main prompt's Maintenance flow).
